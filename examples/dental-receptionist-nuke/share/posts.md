Body:

```text
The idea: an AI receptionist that answers the calls a dental front desk misses and books the appointment, $199 a month per clinic. I'm a solo dev with 10 hours a week and no contacts in dentistry.

Before building I ran it through a research pass (97 sources). It came back PIVOT, 56/100.

What hurt:
- Weave, the phone platform a lot of practices already use, bundles an AI receptionist, serves 40,000+ locations and got endorsed by the ADA this year.
- I counted 10 near-identical AI receptionist launches in the last 12 months.
- r/SideProject and X don't reach dentists. Google Ads for dental keywords runs about $7.85 a click.

What held up:
- Practices already pay $285 to $799 a month for AI answering, and $175 to $700 for human answering services.
- The cheap generic tools ($29 a month) don't support HIPAA, and the dental ones with real booking start around $299.
- The most repeated complaint in competitor reviews is contracts.

The narrowed version: book straight into Open Dental, signed BAA, month to month, sold with a missed-call audit (I call the office at lunch and email the owner what happened). That projects to 60/100, still not a clear go.

Next week I'm calling 40 practices at lunch to count how many don't pick up. I'll post the numbers. If you work in a dental office, how do you handle lunch and after-hours calls today?
```
