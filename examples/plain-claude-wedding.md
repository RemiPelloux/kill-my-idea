Captured on 2026-11-09 with Claude Code, no plugin and no tools (`claude -p --tools ""`), run from an empty folder.
The answer is reproduced unedited, for comparison with [the kill-my-idea report](https://remipelloux.github.io/kill-my-idea/reports/wedding-speeches/).

**Prompt:**

> Is this a good business idea? An AI app that writes personalized wedding speeches for best men and maids of honor, sold at $19 per speech.

**Answer:**

> **It's a decent small business. It's unlikely to become a big one.** The problem is real and people will pay to solve it. The weak spots are differentiation and repeat customers, not demand.
> 
> ## What works
> 
> - **There's real demand at a real moment.** Roughly 2M US weddings a year, each with 1–3 speeches. Most people dread public speaking, so the pain is acute, and it shows up on a deadline.
> - **$19 is easy to say yes to.** People already spend hundreds on wedding-related things. Next to that, $19 feels trivial and buyers won't overthink it.
> - **People search for this.** Queries like "best man speech examples" and "maid of honor speech funny" get steady, high-intent search traffic.
> - **It's cheap to run.** LLM costs per speech are cents, so your margins are almost entirely marketing.
> 
> ## What's hard
> 
> 1. **Your main competitor is free.** Anyone can open ChatGPT or Claude and type "write a best man speech about my brother Jake." Your product has to be clearly better than that, or the only people you'll convert are the ones who haven't thought of it.
> 2. **Customers buy once.** Most people give one or two of these speeches in their life, so lifetime value is about $19. Every sale has to pay for its own acquisition, and paid ads at $19 a sale are tough. You'll mostly depend on SEO and organic social.
> 3. **Others are already doing it.** Template sites, human ghostwriters ($100–500), and several AI speech tools exist. You won't be first.
> 4. **SEO is crowded and shifting.** Big wedding sites (The Knot, Zola) own the top results, and AI search answers are taking a growing share of those clicks.
> 
> ## How to make it beat "just use ChatGPT"
> 
> - **A guided interview, not a prompt box.** Ask the questions people wouldn't think of: how you met, an embarrassing but safe story, the partner's best trait, inside jokes, how long the speech should run, and the crowd (grandma present? roast-friendly?). Most of the quality comes from getting good inputs out of the user.
> - **Delivery coaching.** Read-aloud timing, pause and emphasis markers, a printable cue-card version, maybe AI feedback on a recorded practice run. ChatGPT doesn't do this, and it's what nervous speakers actually want.
> - **Safety checks.** Flag jokes about exes, inside references guests won't get, and anything likely to offend.
> - **Unlimited revisions within a tone you pick** (sentimental, funny, short and sweet).
> 
> ## Ways to grow beyond one-off sales
> 
> - **Expand to other speeches:** father of the bride, groom's speech, eulogies, retirement and milestone birthdays. That's the same engine with a bigger market.
> - **Sell through wedding platforms.** Partnering with Zola, The Knot or wedding planners (as an affiliate or white-label tool) makes customer acquisition far cheaper.
> - **Tiered pricing:** $19 for the basic speech, about $39–49 with coaching and unlimited edits, and a premium tier with a human editor review.
> 
> ## Bottom line
> 
> As a lean side project or bootstrapped niche business, it's reasonable: build an MVP in a few weeks, test SEO and TikTok/Reels content (funny speech clips can spread well), and see whether people convert. As a venture-scale company it's weak, because there's no repeat purchase, the free alternative is strong, and the defensibility is thin.
> 
> **Cheapest way to test it:** put up a landing page with a pre-order button and one or two SEO-targeted pages, and spend about $200 on ads. If you can't get conversions at a cost under about $10 a sale, the economics won't work at $19.
